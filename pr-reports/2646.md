# PR Report: The identity area in the rebuild check

PR: [#2646](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2646)
Base: `main`

## Summary

The rebuild check can now rebuild the identity mirror's 17 tables from empty: `identities`, `profiles`, `address_consolidation_keys`, `consolidations`, `delegations`, `ratings`, `cic_statements` and the mirror's own `identity_chain_*` tables. The rebuilder runs the identity mirror's own code against the run's 6529 chain source up to the mark. Two kinds of rows can't be derived by any rebuild, so they come from files pinned by their SHA-256:

- the identities 6529.io gave random ids before switch-on, with no handle and no chain identity, from the switch-on record (#2640's format);
- the placeholder profiles no chain identity takes, from the file taken at the identity freeze.

Until the real files are taken and pinned, a run reads the test world's own, and a final run refuses them. Publish's and mint's seeds now read the records service's identity from the world instead of writing it. Nothing outside the rebuild check, its test world and its docs changes.

## What Changed

- **The rebuilder** (`identity-chain-mirror.rebuild.ts`, step `identity.chain-mirror`).
  - Its first act loads the switch-on record (`switch-on-record.ts`): the identities rows and their address keys, read with #2640's parser and checked against their pins. It runs before the mirror replays, so the mirror adopts the same ids for chain identities on the same consolidation key (#2636). It isn't a seed, because seeds run before the rebuild empties the identity tables.
  - It then runs the identity mirror, as the delegations loop runs it, until it reaches the chain source's finalized height, which must be the mark. Profile documents come from the run's files source under their CID names, checked against the CID.
  - Once caught up with the chain, it runs the mirror on until the walk that gives the rows off the chain a level of REP + TDH has reached the end of the identities, as the delegations loop's runs do over time (see "The level walk at scale" below).
  - Last, it restores each placeholder profile no chain identity took (`placeholder-restore.ts`): its profile, and its identities and address-key rows without the wallets the chain binds. Their TDH columns start at 0. The file's reader checks the whole file before anything is written, and refuses it on any line that isn't a placeholder, a profile, wallet or identity wallet given twice, or a count that differs. The restore refuses a placeholder whose profile id another profiles row already holds.
  - As the writer of `identities`, `profiles`, `address_consolidation_keys` and `identity_chain_principals`, it names their readers' rebuilders in its `before`: `holdings.ethereum`, `personal.settings`, `subs.legacy-history`, `subs.top-ups`, `waves.chain-mirror` and `tdh.chain-days`. The five not yet in the tree show as unresolved cross-area edges.
- **The pins** (`SWITCH_ON_RECORD_PINS`, `FREEZE_PLACEHOLDERS_SHA256`). Both are null until the real files are taken. Until then the world's seed writes the world's record, with a pins file, and the world's placeholders file into the run's seed files, and a final run refuses both.
- **The seed** (`identity.probe-profiles`, `rebuild-world/site-rows.ts`). It puts the stack in its state before the rebuild, writing 6529.io's rows through the code that writes them in production.
  - Before switch-on: three placeholder profiles (the founder rule takes one, no chain identity takes the other two), a TDH-only identity, and an address-only identity on the founder's wallet with a fixed id.
  - Then it takes the switch-on record from those rows, both of its reads in one REPEATABLE READ transaction, and runs the mirror to the mark. The founder's chain identity has that wallet alone, so the mirror adopts the address-only row's id, here and again in the rebuild.
  - After switch-on: TDH on an imported identity and on an untaken placeholder.
- **The world** (`rebuild-world/world-ids.ts`). A devnet's identity world, captured in the shared gateway recording beside the waves world. Its wallets are the devnet's test wallets, derived from their labels; a test pins them to the capture. With the waves world present, the seed gives four of its people 6529.io TDH equal to their chain TDH at the mark's TDH day (20782).
- **Planted references** (`rebuild-world/placeholder-references.ts`, seed `identity.placeholder-references`). One reference of each kind (follows, mutes, notifications, reactions, settings, moderation, proxies, profile sites and the rest) to an untaken placeholder, after `identity.actors-check`. Probes read the follows, a notification, proxies and activity logs through them.
- **Plans** (`identity.manifest.ts`), until 2026-11-30:
  - planned rows: the `identities` and `address_consolidation_keys` rows the switch-on record carries, selected as the record selects them, less the ids the mirrors derive from an address after switch-on;
  - a column plan on `identities` (`tdh`, `basetdh_rate`): two seeded site rows and the four waves people, which the rebuild leaves at 0 until the TDH area writes identities' TDH from the chain.
- **The records service** (`publish.seed.ts`, `mint-log-rebuild.seed.ts`, `records-service.ts`). Both seeds read the records service's profile by wallet from the world, and both manifests' records-service flags name its profile and wallet. The world actors' guard test follows: no seed writes the records service's profile, and the placeholder restore is listed as the rebuild's one new `profiles` writer.
- **Docs** (`docs/rebuild-check.md`, `docs/architecture.md`). The identity area, its files and pins, its plans and how they end.

## Risks / Follow-Ups

- **A final run fails until three follow-ups land.** A final run fails on any plan, and refuses the world's files in place of the pinned ones.
  - The switch-on record: the 6529.io team's take, about 01:00 UTC on the switch-on day, put in the files source and pinned. The planned-rows plans end after a run on it with nothing differing.
  - The placeholders file: taken at the identity freeze, then pinned.
  - TDH: the column plan ends when the TDH area's `tdh.chain-days` rebuilder writes identities' TDH from the chain.
- **Untaken placeholders' sites.** A site owned by a placeholder no chain identity takes isn't rebuilt (the `cms_sites` difference above). A follow-up carries those sites in a content-addressed source the rebuild reads.
- **Unresolved cross-area edges.** Five of the rebuilder's `before` steps aren't in the tree yet. The registry test pins them, each resolves when its rebuilder lands, and a final run fails on them until then.
- **The level walk at scale.** Each mirror run walks 20,000 `identities` rows (20 chunks of 1,000 keys). While the mirror runs, the table holds the switch-on record's rows and the rows the mirror writes for chain identities. The placeholders' rows aren't in it: they are restored afterwards, with `level_raw` = REP, the walk's rule with TDH at 0.
  - So the walk needs ceil(N / 20,000) runs for N rows, one more when it starts mid-table: 6 runs for 100,000 rows, 16 for 300,000, 57 for 1.1 million. N is unknown until the team's record and the chain's identities exist.
  - These walk runs come after the five runs that catch up with the chain. They are held to ceil(N / 20,000) + 1, with N counted once; each must move the walk's cursor, or the step fails; none starts after 10 minutes; and each one's time is logged. A walk run's time is an estimate (a few seconds), not measured: at 1.1 million rows the 57 runs fit the 10 minutes only at under about 10.5 s a run, and the first run on real data logs the figure.
- **Placeholders at scale.** 1,019,000 made-up placeholders took 28 s to list and 10 minutes to restore on one connection, and the file is now read through once more before each, so nothing is written from a file that fails a check. The mirror's runs take at most 47 minutes (35 to catch up, 12 for the walk), so the rebuilder's timeout is 75 minutes, and the seed's, which also runs the mirror, 50.
- **A capture on another TDH day** needs the four waves people's TDH values moved with it.
