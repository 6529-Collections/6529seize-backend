# PR Report: Rebuild check: subscriptions rebuilt from the opening files, the journal and Ethereum

PR: [#2571](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2571)
Base: #2547's branch; merges after #2547, #2542, #2569 and #2551

## Summary

The subscriptions area joins the rebuild check. Its seeds put the stack where the identity subscription tables are the truth, through the paths production uses and in the world's order: subscriptions and card 1's list first, then the holdings seed mints the cards, then card 1's airdrop is charged. Its rebuilders then rebuild, from empty:

- the identity tables, from the opening files go-live published and the journal in the shared record log, read from the records wave and its segment files, with each choice's signed record head put back;
- the legacy tables (the rollback copy, which routes that don't switch still read), from the identity tables;
- the top-up rows, and the queue's held-back top-ups, from Ethereum through the top-up loop's own discovery and rule.

The check compares the tables and the subscription routes' answers before and after.

## What Changed

- **Seeds** (`src/rebuild/areas/subs.seed.ts`):
  - `subs.seed`, before the holdings seed: a top-up from each probe wallet as a real transfer on the run's anvil, found and credited by the top-up loop's discovery; the transactions loop's processed block at the chain's head; all editions for the first wallet; and a wide identity of 15 placeholder wallets (`subs.wide-identity.ts`), whose real top-up the key-width guard queues whole.
  - `subs.switch`, before the holdings seed: the wide identity's principal; `go-live` into the run's seed files, which credits the queued top-up; a top-up and a signed choice after the switch, the choice through the write route's own writer under the signed-records fixture grant; the daily build of card 1's list, published into the seed files; card 1's first wallet pulled into "Phase 0" on both sides, journaled; a checkpoint and the journal's closed segment in the records wave, with each head drop's wave and dropper metrics, as drop creation writes them.
  - `subs.redemptions`, after the holdings seed and before the mint seed: the transactions loop's redemption charges card 1's airdrop on both sides, then a checkpoint and the journal's new segment.
  - Out of that order the switch and redemption seeds fail and say why. Every seed closes its own record log entries.
- **Rebuilders:**
  - `subs.identity` (`src/subscriptions-identity/opening/rebuild-check.steps.ts`): the production `rebuildIdentityTables` from the files source and the records wave, then the switch the journal records, at its time. It writes its partition of `signed_record_heads`: `(family, rtype)` in the four `(ps, subs.*)` choice types.
  - `subs.legacy-history` (`src/subscriptions-identity/opening/legacy-copy.ts`), one step: final rows, list uploads and redemptions; then balances, modes and choices under each identity's consolidation key. It leaves out, and counts, the keys wider than the narrowest legacy key column, the key-width guard's width rule.
  - `subs.top-ups` (`src/subscriptionsTopUpLoop/top-ups-rebuild-check.steps.ts`), after the copy: discovery from genesis to the mark, putting each top-up where `persistTopUps` puts it: its row, or the whole top-up queued when the guard holds its key back (the queue's TOP_UP partition). No credit, no cursor.
- **Production code:**
  - `discoverTopUps` takes options (a persist step, whether the cursor moves, a last block); none given, nothing changes. New `recordTopUps` writes the rows alone, through `persistTopUps`' own key, lock and guard check (moved into `topUpTarget` and `queueTopUp`, which both call).
  - `updateSubscriptions` takes an uploader for the published list; none given, the list goes to Arweave as today.
  - go-live journals its opening entry at the switch time, the time it writes into `switch_at`, instead of the moment `journal()` ran, so a rebuild can put the switch back exactly.
- **Manifest** (`src/rebuild/manifests/subs.manifest.ts`): the steps, their `needs`, `before` and the heads partition; the records service flags; volatile columns with reasons; the top-up probe leaves out `created_at`; `subscriptions_logs` is EXEMPT; `subscription_deferred_writes` is INDEX, its TOP_UP rows rebuilt by `subs.top-ups`.
- **Tests:** the seeds, `subs.identity` and `subs.legacy-history` against MySQL, end to end with their tables emptied; `subs.top-ups` through its write and discovery tests; the records wave's drop metrics against its drops; the wide identity's queued top-up, credited once at the switch, and its wallets' keys; the copy's width rule and its limit; the order guards; `recordTopUps` queues exactly what `persistTopUps` queues; a wide-key top-up credited once, live and across a rebuild; discovery's options; the uploader by default and given.

## Backend Impact

**Deploy:** none of its own; it ships with #2547's code. No migration, flag, route, schedule, queue or alarm change. The top-up loop and the daily build run changed code whose defaults behave as today. The `subscriptions:opening` CLI's go-live writes its opening entry at the switch time.

**Live behaviour.** Unchanged, apart from the opening entry's time, which matters only once go-live runs.

## Risks / Follow-Ups

- **The identity mirror's table.** Main creates `identity_chain_principals`, so no step waits for it.
- **The mint area.** Its rebuild steps read tables this PR's rebuilders write: the mint log sets the phase columns of the final rows `subs.identity` and `subs.legacy-history` create, and the mint stats read `subscriptions_redeemed`, which `subs.legacy-history` rebuilds. Once those steps are in main, the subs steps declare `before:` edges to them; the registry refuses an edge to a step it doesn't know.
- **What the rebuild can't reproduce:** legacy keys the opening merged, keys whose wallets span identities, the rows' own write times, a top-up row's discovery time, legacy rows a failed dual write left different, and the keys the key-width guard holds back. In its queue, only the top-ups of keys too wide for the legacy columns come back, as pending: not the other kinds, consolidations queued after the switch, or replayed rows. Production makes the other kinds only for a key that still has legacy rows and that the guard holds back (too long for a legacy column, or with a write waiting), from those legacy rows, which the identity tables don't keep; a wide identity has none. A fitting key with an earlier write still waiting gets its legacy rows copied, which production holds until the replay, and a key over 567 characters with legacy rows from before the guard keeps them in production, which the copy leaves out.
- **Legacy readers.** Mint stats, the ETH price recovery and the public coverage route still read the legacy tables after the switch, and the mint log's legacy part reads and rebuilds the legacy final rows. #2547 moved the distributions, minting claims and community mint metrics readers to the table the switch names. A follow-up moves coverage to the identity tables.
- **The fixture grant.** The switch seed signs its choice under the signed-records test fixtures' app grant (a well-known test wallet), only in the rebuild check.
- **The wide identity's rows.** Its identities, profiles, address_consolidation_keys and identity_chain_principals rows are written by a subs seed, as the records service's are. They belong in the identity area's probe fixture once that area rebuilds them.
