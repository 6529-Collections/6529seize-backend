# Mint log: Drop Forge's own writes to the minting tables are logged

[Backend PR #2645](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2645)

## Summary

Drop Forge's worker wrote three kinds of minting rows outside the mint log: a phase it prepares in the background (the subscriptions' phase columns, the phase's distribution rows and its merkle root and proofs), its finalize (the normalized rows), and the completion flags it mirrors into a claim's actions. Each is now logged as a service write (`authority: 'drop-forge'`) inside the transaction Drop Forge already runs it in, after Drop Forge's own locks. A rebuild from the log alone reproduces a card Drop Forge prepared, finalized and flagged.

It builds on #2536 (the mint log) and #2561 (the mint log's rebuild check steps), both in main.

## Backend impact

- **Changed functions:** `api` (the mint log's capture, and the signer transaction for Drop Forge's operations and recovery requests), `memesDistributionHandler` (phase preparation and finalize) and `dropForgeLaunchHandler` (completion flags and the signer transaction). No route or response changes, no migration. Other functions that bundle the mint log's recorder pick up one change on their next deploy and need none now: for a collaboration write inside a caller's transaction, a shadow failure's or a mismatch's alert is sent once that transaction commits.
- **With the mint log flags off**, Drop Forge runs as it does today: nothing is logged, each completion flag is written where it is mirrored, a flag that fails to write blocks the launch, and a launch completes in the pass that writes its last flag. The minting-claims API's code is as on main.
- **With the mint log on**, Drop Forge's writes read their kind's parts with FOR UPDATE and hold the mint log's head until their transaction commits. In primary mode a mint log failure fails the job step, or undoes a held flag with its entry.
  - Completion flags are written at the end of the signer transaction, each under its own savepoint, at most two a pass; a third waits for the next pass.
  - A flag that fails to write doesn't block the launch. It is retried on a later pass, before the launch completes (a paused or blocked launch only once it runs again), and its first failure posts one event in the Drop Forge reports wave, with the alert mention only once the launch has ended. The event names the claim action route and body to set it by hand.
  - A launch completes on the pass after its last flags are written, at most about 15 seconds later. An admin's change to the same claim action during a pass is overwritten by the flag (the last writer wins, as before, over a longer window).
  - The phase's cache eviction runs after the job's commit, which waits for it.
  - The held flags take the mint log's one head at the end of the signer transaction. Drop Forge has one signer, so two passes' flags never wait for each other; the head may be held by a phase step on another card ({PHASE_STEP} on a 4,000-wallet card), an admin's split or another logged mint write. A pass's flags share one lock-wait budget of 15 s (`HELD_FLAG_LOCK_WAIT_SECONDS`): the first may wait all of it, the second what is left, but at least 1 s. In primary mode a flag that gives up is unmirrored and written on a later pass; in shadow mode it commits unlogged with a `[MINT_LOG_SHADOW_FAILED]` alert, a gap the genesis before primary covers.
  - Each held flag captures the whole card four times with the replay check on. On a card with 4,000 allowlisted wallets that added about 5.1 s to a signer pass in the DB benchmark; the pass took 20 ms with the mint log off.
  - So on such a card the held flags add at most about 20 s to a pass with one flag and 26 s with two (15 s of waiting, 1 s for the second flag, 5.1 s of captures each), on top of the pass's own chain reads. Meanwhile requests that take the signer row (retry, manual-transaction operations, recovery requests) wait behind any pass, the launch controls (pause, cancel, resume, arm) behind a pass on their launch, and the Automatic launch switch behind a tick pass. An API request through API Gateway may last 29 s; one that waits longer gets a 504, and its change still applies once the pass commits.
  - For a write inside the caller's transaction (all of Drop Forge's), a failed shadow entry's priority alert and an entry's mismatch alert are sent after that transaction commits, which waits for them; the error is logged at once.

## Backend deployment

`api`, `memesDistributionHandler` and `dropForgeLaunchHandler`, in any order (#2536 and #2561 are already in main).

The mint log's primary switch waits until this PR has been in production for 14 hours. Until this PR is deployed, the mint log can run in shadow mode; Drop Forge's writes are then missing from it, and the new genesis before primary covers them.

Rollback: redeploy the previous three functions, in any order.

**Team asks:** a review by Drop Forge's owners, and a deploy at a time that suits Drop Forge's jobs. With the mint log flags off, Drop Forge runs as it does today (above).

Also: current reads for Drop Forge's writes only, both orders against an admin's logged write, held flags across a slow chain call, a held flag failing in shadow and primary mode, misuse refused.
- `src/drop-forge/drop-forge-worker.db.test.ts`: with the mint log off, today's behaviour (the flag written in the completing pass, a failed flag blocking the launch); with it on, a flag failing in a completing pass, in a receipt's pass and in a reserving pass, through the worker's tick.

- `src/mint-log/mint-log-drop-forge.db.test.ts` also: the head held past the 15 s budget in primary and shadow mode, two flags in one pass behind a held head (the pass waits about 16 s, not 30), a flag waiting behind another card's logged write and writing within the budget, and a benchmark (run with `MINT_LOG_TIMING=1`) of the phase step, finalize and one held flag on a card with 4,000 allowlisted wallets.
- Unit tests for the recorder (every capture of a memes phase write gets the subscriptions switch's answer and the kind's parts; a shadow failure's and a mismatch's alert after the commit), nothing held with the mint log off, the held flags' shared lock-wait budget and per-pass cap, an undone flag's after-commit work dropped, their "transaction gone" path, preparation and completion.

## Known limits

- With the mint log on, a flag that fails on every pass keeps a launch whose actions are all settled running, retried every 15 seconds; setting it by hand doesn't stop the retries (a follow-up).
- Every logged write captures the whole card, so a held flag costs seconds on a large card. The follow-up is to capture only the parts a write's kind may change.
- The rebuild check's seed writes Drop Forge's kinds through the API's paths only; a held-flag seed step is the second follow-up.
- In shadow mode, once #2547's subscriptions switch names the identity tables, a phase split whose head lock timed out runs unlogged and can deadlock with a Drop Forge job. InnoDB rolls one back: the admin gets a 500 and runs the split again, or the job is retried on its next pass. The unlogged split is a gap in the log; a new genesis (`6529 run mint-log:genesis -- --live --chain mint`) clears it, and the primary switch waits for that.
