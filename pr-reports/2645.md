# Mint log: Drop Forge's own writes to the minting tables are logged

[Backend PR #2645](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2645)

## Summary

Drop Forge's worker wrote three kinds of minting rows outside the mint log: a phase it prepares in the background (the subscriptions' phase columns, the phase's distribution rows and its merkle root and proofs), its finalize (the normalized rows), and the completion flags it mirrors into a claim's actions. Each is now logged as a service write (`authority: 'drop-forge'`) inside the transaction Drop Forge already runs it in, after Drop Forge's own locks. A rebuild from the log alone reproduces a card Drop Forge prepared, finalized and flagged.

It builds on #2536 (the mint log) and #2561 (the mint log's rebuild check steps), both in main.

## Backend impact

- **Changed functions:** `api` (the mint log's capture, and the signer transaction for Drop Forge's operations and recovery requests), `memesDistributionHandler` (phase preparation and finalize) and `dropForgeLaunchHandler` (completion flags and the signer transaction). No route or response changes, no migration. Other functions that bundle the mint log's recorder pick up one change on their next deploy and need none now: for a collaboration write inside a caller's transaction, a shadow failure's or a mismatch's alert is sent once that transaction commits.
- **With the mint log flags off**, Drop Forge runs as it does today: nothing is logged, each completion flag is written where it is mirrored, a flag that fails to write blocks the launch, and a launch completes in the pass that writes its last flag. The minting-claims API's code is as on main.
- **With the mint log on**, Drop Forge's writes read their kind's parts with FOR UPDATE and hold the mint log's head until their transaction commits. In primary mode a mint log failure fails the job step, or undoes a held flag with its entry.
  - Completion flags are written at the end of the signer transaction, each under its own savepoint, at most two a pass. The cap only delays a flag.
  - A flag that fails to write, or that the pass has no room for, doesn't block the launch. A later delivery writes it, in any state: the launch keeps getting deliveries while a completed group's flag is missing. On a launch that isn't running, the retry inserts the row only where the claim has none. An admin unsets a flag by writing a row with completed false, and nothing deletes rows, so an admin's row stays as it is.
  - A flag's first miss posts one plain event in the Drop Forge reports wave, and every failure logs an error. A completed or cancelled launch that still lacks the flag 10 minutes later posts one alert, with the mention, naming the claim action route and body to set it by hand. Nothing new schedules deliveries for old launches.
  - A launch completes on the pass after its last flags are written, at most about 15 seconds later. An admin's change to the same claim action during a pass is overwritten by the flag (the last writer wins, as before, over a longer window).
  - The phase's cache eviction runs after the job's commit, which waits for it.
  - Measured on a card with 4,000 allowlisted wallets, with the replay check on: a logged phase step holds the mint log's one head 6.5 s, finalize 6.3 s, and each held flag adds 5.1 s to a signer pass (11 ms with the mint log off).
  - The held flags take that head at the end of the signer transaction. Drop Forge has one signer, so two passes' flags never wait for each other; the head may be held by a phase step on another card, finalize, an admin's split or another logged mint write.
  - A pass's flags share one lock-wait budget of 10 s (`HELD_FLAG_LOCK_WAIT_SECONDS`), about 1.5 times the longest holder: the first may wait all of it, the second what is left, but at least 1 s. In primary mode a flag that gives up is written by a later delivery. In shadow mode it commits unlogged with a `[MINT_LOG_SHADOW_FAILED]` alert, a gap the genesis before primary covers. Bigger cards give up more often: no loss in primary, a gap each time in shadow.
  - How long a pass can hold the signer. Before its flags it reads the chain, as on main: 3 RPCs for the latest receipt, 3 more with a recovery winner, 3 more with a confirmed manual transaction, about 4 per pending manual transaction, each with a 10 s deadline. The flags add at most 21 s (10 s of waiting, 1 s for the second flag, 5.1 s of captures each).

    | Case | Chain reads | Flags | Pass |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Typical launch, every RPC at its deadline | 30 s | 21 s | about 51 s |
    | With a recovery winner and a confirmed manual transaction, every RPC at its deadline | 90 s | 21 s | about 111 s |
    | Normal RPCs | under 1 s | 10 s, up to 21 s behind a phase step | about 11 s, up to about 22 s |

    The reads beyond 29 s exist on main already; this PR adds at most 21 s.
  - What waits behind a pass:
    - requests that take the signer row (retry, manual-transaction operations, recovery requests), behind any pass;
    - the launch controls (pause, cancel, resume, arm), behind a pass on their launch;
    - the Automatic launch switch, behind a tick pass or a receipt pass's rebroadcast check;
    - every logged mint write on any card (an admin's claim action, phase split, airdrop upload, claim edit), for about 5.1 s per flag.

    An API request through API Gateway may last 29 s; one that waits longer gets a 504, and its change still applies once the pass commits.
  - For a write inside the caller's transaction (all of Drop Forge's), a failed or skipped shadow entry's priority alert and an entry's mismatch alert are sent after that transaction commits, which waits for them; the error is logged at once.

## Backend deployment

`api`, `memesDistributionHandler` and `dropForgeLaunchHandler`, in any order (#2536 and #2561 are already in main).

The mint log's primary switch waits until this PR has been in production for 14 hours. Until this PR is deployed, the mint log can run in shadow mode; Drop Forge's writes are then missing from it, and the new genesis before primary covers them.

Rollback: redeploy the previous three functions, in any order.

**Team asks:** a review by Drop Forge's owners, and a deploy at a time that suits Drop Forge's jobs. With the mint log flags off, Drop Forge runs as it does today (above).

## Known limits

- With the mint log on, a flag that fails on every pass keeps its launch getting a delivery every 15 seconds; on a running launch, setting it by hand doesn't stop the retries (a follow-up).
- In shadow mode, a flag that gives up on the head commits unlogged; deferring it to the next delivery as in primary mode is a follow-up.
- Every logged write captures the whole card, so a held flag costs seconds on a large card. The follow-up is to capture only the parts a write's kind may change.
- The rebuild check's seed writes Drop Forge's kinds through the API's paths only; a held-flag seed step is the second follow-up.
- In shadow mode, once #2547's subscriptions switch names the identity tables, a phase split whose head lock timed out runs unlogged and can deadlock with a Drop Forge job. InnoDB rolls one back: the admin gets a 500 and runs the split again, or the job is retried on its next pass. The unlogged split is a gap in the log; a new genesis (`6529 run mint-log:genesis -- --live --chain mint`) clears it, and the primary switch waits for that.
