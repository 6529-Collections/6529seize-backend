# Mint log: Drop Forge's own writes to the minting tables are logged

[Backend PR #2645](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2645)

## Summary

Drop Forge's worker wrote three kinds of minting rows outside the mint log: a phase it prepares in the background (the subscriptions' phase columns, the phase's distribution rows and its merkle root and proofs), its finalize (the normalized rows), and the completion flags it mirrors into a claim's actions. Each is now logged as a service write (`authority: 'drop-forge'`) inside the transaction Drop Forge already runs it in, after Drop Forge's own locks. A rebuild from the log alone reproduces a card Drop Forge prepared, finalized and flagged.

It builds on #2536 (the mint log) and #2561 (the mint log's rebuild check steps), both in main.

## Backend impact

- **Changed functions:** `api` (the mint log's capture and the merkle delete, and the signer transaction for Drop Forge's operations and recovery requests), `memesDistributionHandler` (phase preparation and finalize) and `dropForgeLaunchHandler` (completion flags and the signer transaction). No route or response changes, no migration.
- **With the mint log flags off**, nothing is logged, and these change:
  - completion flags are written at the end of the signer transaction, each under its own savepoint;
  - a flag that fails to write no longer blocks the launch. It is retried on a later pass, before the launch completes (a paused or blocked launch only once it runs again), and shown once as a warning event in the Drop Forge reports wave. The warning names the claim action route and body to set it by hand;
  - a launch completes on the pass after its last flags are written, at most about 15 seconds later;
  - an admin's change to the same claim action during a pass is overwritten by the flag (the last writer wins, as before, over a longer window);
  - a phase's old merkle roots are read with a lock before they and their proofs are deleted (hardening);
  - the phase's cache eviction runs after the job's commit, which waits for it.
- **With the mint log on**, Drop Forge's writes read their kind's parts with FOR UPDATE and hold the mint log's head until their transaction commits. In primary mode a mint log failure fails the job step, or undoes a held flag with its entry.
  - The held flags take the mint log's one head at the end of the signer transaction, with the signer's, the settings' and the launch's rows locked. A pass's flags share one lock-wait budget of 15 s (`HELD_FLAG_LOCK_WAIT_SECONDS`), about three times one flag's captures on a large card: the first flag may wait all of it, each later one what is left, but at least 1 s. In primary mode a flag that gives up is unmirrored and written on a later pass; in shadow mode it commits unlogged with a `[MINT_LOG_SHADOW_FAILED]` alert, a gap the genesis before primary covers.
  - Each held flag captures the whole card four times with the replay check on. On a card with 4,000 allowlisted wallets that added about 5.1 s to a signer pass in the DB test, about 10 s for a Phase 2 completion's two flags; the pass took 11 ms with the mint log off. The signer's and the launch's rows stay locked meanwhile.
  - The longest a pass can then hold the signer on such a card: the 15 s budget, plus 1 s for each flag after the first, plus about 5.1 s for each flag that gets the head. One flag: about 20 s; a Phase 2 completion: about 26 s. Turning Automatic launch off, or pausing that launch, waits behind the pass for up to that long, close to the 29 s an API request through API Gateway may last.
  - For a write inside the caller's transaction (all of Drop Forge's), a failed shadow entry's priority alert and an entry's mismatch alert are sent after that transaction commits, which waits for them; the error is logged at once.

## Backend deployment

`api`, `memesDistributionHandler` and `dropForgeLaunchHandler`, in any order (#2536 and #2561 are already in main).

The mint log's primary switch waits until this PR has been in production for 14 hours. Until this PR is deployed, the mint log can run in shadow mode; Drop Forge's writes are then missing from it, and the new genesis before primary covers them.

Rollback: redeploy the previous three functions, in any order.

**Team asks:** a review by Drop Forge's owners, and a deploy at a time that suits Drop Forge's jobs. They are told what changes with the flags off (above).

## Tests

- `src/mint-log/mint-log-drop-forge.db.test.ts`: each Drop Forge write logged, with the rebuild from its entries alone matching the live card. Also: current reads for Drop Forge's writes only, the early-snapshot delete, both orders against an admin's logged write, held flags across a slow chain call, a held flag failing in shadow and primary mode, misuse refused.
- `src/drop-forge/drop-forge-worker.db.test.ts`: a flag failing in a completing pass, in a receipt's pass and in a reserving pass, through the worker's tick.
- `src/mint-log/mint-log-drop-forge.db.test.ts` also: the head held past the 15 s budget in primary and shadow mode, two flags in one pass behind a held head (the pass waits about 16 s, not 30), two cards' held flags at once (the second waits and writes within the budget), and the timing of one held flag on a card with 4,000 allowlisted wallets.
- Unit tests for the recorder (every capture of a memes phase write gets the subscriptions switch's answer and the kind's parts; a shadow failure's and a mismatch's alert after the commit), the held flags' shared lock-wait budget, an undone flag's after-commit work dropped, their "transaction gone" path, preparation and completion.

## Known limits

- A flag that fails on every pass keeps a launch whose actions are all settled running, retried every 15 seconds; setting it by hand doesn't stop the retries.
- Every logged write captures the whole card, so a held flag costs seconds on a large card. The follow-up is to capture only the parts a write's kind may change.
- The rebuild check's seed writes Drop Forge's kinds through the API's paths only; a held-flag seed step is the second follow-up.
- In shadow mode, once #2547's subscriptions switch names the identity tables, a phase split whose head lock timed out runs unlogged and can deadlock with a Drop Forge job. InnoDB rolls one back: the admin gets a 500 and runs the split again, or the job is retried on its next pass. The unlogged split is a gap in the log; a new genesis (`6529 run mint-log:genesis -- --live --chain mint`) clears it, and the primary switch waits for that.
