# Collect rules as signed records, with the server's facts in the record log

[Backend PR #2566](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2566) · stacked on [#2546](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-backend/pull/2546)

## Summary

Collect rules leave MySQL as their only home, on the machinery #2546 adds for personal settings: `PERSONAL_RECORDS_MODE`, the freeze, the one-time import and the rebuild. Plans are scan jobs the page makes again when it needs them, so they stay in MySQL as kept (ephemeral) data.

Two parties change a rule, and each signs what only it knows:
- **The person signs their intent:** a sealed `collect.rule` record that creates a rule, pauses it or resumes it. The terms can't change. A record finds a rule saved through today's route by the Idempotency-Key it was created with. Found that way, it is the create sent again: accepted when its terms match, it leaves the rule's state alone. One that would pause the rule is refused: a rule is paused by its id. Terms are compared in their stored form (wallets lowercased, targets in asset order).
- **The server logs its facts** in the record log's `collect` lane, on the private chain, in the transaction that makes each one:
  - a review reserved;
  - a transaction sent, with its nonce once Ethereum shows it as the rule's purchase;
  - a settlement;
  - a release.

A rebuild restores the import, then replays the lane in logged order, through the same store functions and live's checks. It checks each rule change against the person's record, and each settlement and release against Ethereum.

**Nothing changes until `PERSONAL_RECORDS_MODE=primary`.** In `legacy` and `shadow` the rule routes work as today, and nothing new is logged outside the switch's freeze.

## Backend impact

- **Changed routes, primary only:**
  - `POST /collect/rules` and `/collect/rules/{id}/pause` answer 409 `PERSONAL_RECORDS_REQUIRED`; the client signs `collect.rule` instead.
  - Reconcile settles or releases a review whose market operation is gone. It settles from Ethereum after checking the transaction is the reviewed purchase, including one sped up or replaced at the same nonce. It releases only what can never be mined.
  - Prepare answers 409 `RULE_PENDING` instead of 404 for such a review. For a purchase sent and not mined, it names the nonce to replace.
- **All modes:** while frozen, the four rule routes answer 503 `PERSONAL_SETTINGS_PAUSED`.
- **The market submit route:** in primary and during the freeze, the rule asks Ethereum for the sent transaction after the market has kept the hash. Whatever the rule does with it, the submit answers as the market does; a refusal is logged as an error.
- **Schema:** two nullable columns, `collect_rule_operations.transaction_hash` and `transaction_nonce`.
- No new routes, tables, loops or flags.

## Known limits

- **An order sent whose hash never reached 6529.io**, then caught by a rebuild, is released once its quote expires. If it was mined, the rule doesn't count it. This needs the hash never submitted, the journal lost and the purchase mined, all together.
- **A dropped transaction with no later activity from the funding wallet** stays pending until its nonce is used. The way out is a replacement (cancel) at that nonce, which prepare's 409 names.
- **An unchecked hash that turns out to be another transaction** is refused at reconcile and never gets a nonce. The rule's prepare stays blocked until support clears it.
- **Historical state:** finding the transaction at a used nonce needs the RPC provider to serve historical state.
- **EIP-7702:** another account's transaction can use the funding wallet's nonce through a set-code authorization. The search then finds no transaction from the wallet at that nonce, and the purchase stays pending; it is never released wrongly. Support clears it, as in the other blocked case.
- **A possible follow-up:** start the nonce search at the review's block rather than block 0. Today a release costs about 29 RPC calls.
- **Hashes with no nonce:** a hash kept before this ships has no nonce, and settles when mined, as today.
